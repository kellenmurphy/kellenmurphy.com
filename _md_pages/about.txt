# About Me

![Kellen Murphy at work with his dog Lucy](https://kellenmurphy.com/assets/images/pandemic_office_mate.jpg)

This is me. This is what I look like on the day-to-day at work. Thanks to COVID, I'm solidly #WFH forever... That's (one of) my office-mate(s), Lucy. She's mad because I spent 30 minutes explaining to yet another client that `urn:oasis:names:tc:SAML:1.1:nameid-format:unspecified` is **NOT** a literal format specification string that you needed to supply in your `<SAMLResponse>`, but rather a clarifying statement that you **needn't specify** a format rather than giving her treats!

I am an Identity Architect with the [University of Virginia](https://identity.virginia.edu). My specialization is single sign-on technologies, namely SAML and OpenID Connect, and I serve as the local subject matter expert for all things authentication and authorization related, including:

- leading enterprise identity infrastructure like Shibboleth and Grouper,
- helping the University manage the components of its SSO and IAM environment,
- and bringing my expertise to bear on complex architectural challenges related to identity.

If you have an IAM issue... I'm a good person to discuss that issue with!

I peaked in cuteness at age 3... it's been downhill since then. I wanted to be an architect as a child, mostly because I liked [Lego](https://lego.com) and the adults around me shrugged at that and said, "Hey, you could design buildings!" Unfortunately, architects need to know how to draw, so I turned to the other aspect of buildings... structural integrity. That led me for a long time to think I'd be an engineer of some sort, and I had a knack at math and science, so it fit.

At some point along my studies of science, however, I stopped caring about anything other than an unabashed search for knowledge. I wanted to master my understanding of the most fundamental nature of what, exactly, was this thing we called "matter." Unsurprisingly, therefore, I studied physics... first at [Otterbein College](https://otterbein.edu) (now Otterbein University), where I pivoted all of my studies toward high-energy nuclear physics, a.k.a. particle physics. I also picked up a degree in math because "might as well."

After completing my undergrad work, I chose to attend [Ohio University](https://ohio.edu) (note: **not** Ohio *State* University). OU didn't really offer high-energy physics in their nuclear theory group, so I instead worked on what could generously be called "intermediate"-energy physics. I worked with Dr. Daniel Phillips to complete an M.S. thesis on the topic of [effective field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effective_field_theory). Specifically, we computed the electromagnetic form factors of the light nuclei: ²H and ³He.

The math behind that work basically burned me out on quantum chromodynamics, field theory, and (very) advanced calculus; so I knew pretty quickly on in my graduate school career that nuclear physics as a path wasn't right for me. Instead of looking at the nature of matter at the smallest scales, I chose to look for answers instead at the largest, and began studying observational cosmology under Dr. Doug Clowe.

I began working on weak gravitational lensing, which culminated in my dissertation in December 2013 titled [Constraining Cosmology with Weak Gravitational Lensing](https://etd.ohiolink.edu/apexprod/rws_etd/send_file/send?accession=ohiou1384957353&disposition=inline). I used cross correlation tomography of weak lensing signals to study to what extent dark matter in galaxy clusters bends the light from background galaxies at different redshifts. If we look at how this change in ellipticity varies with the lensed object's distance (effectively the same thing as redshift) we had hoped to build an effective constraint on the dark energy equation of state. The gist of my research was that weak lensing wasn't a very good tool for trying to constrain the cosmological parameters related to dark energy, but in science negative results are good too! Anything that furthers our understanding is **good science**.

![Kellen and Holly](https://kellenmurphy.com/assets/images/holly_and_i.jpg)

While I was in grad school, I met the love of my life, Holly. We married in 2009, and had our twin daughters Hope and Lily in 2012. Right about the same time I was thinking about defending a dissertation and moving on to the next phase of my academic life. The only post-doc opportunities I was seriously considering were in Europe, which is a long way from Athens, OH. Since we really needed help from our local families raising our kids, I decided that an academic career wasn't going to be for me, and that instead I should look for "real" work.

I was looking at jobs locally as a developer, and given that there weren't many options for that kind of work in Athens, I started a business to consolidate my freelancing work, along with picking up some other small-business IT projects locally. In 2016, BCS Engineering acquired my business, mainly to acquire me. I had gotten to know Brandon and Carrie well through our church, where both Brandon and I were active volunteers with the technical teams. I began working for Brandon and Carrie, leading their local small-business IT consulting efforts, and moonlighting on some programming projects for the e-commerce side of the business. I wasn't a PHP developer, but I rather quickly became one, gaining substantial experience in the e-commerce market working on the [X-cart](https://xcart.com) and [Magento / Adobe Commerce](https://business.adobe.com/products/magento/magento-commerce.html) platforms.

![Hope and Lily](https://kellenmurphy.com/assets/images/hope_and_lily.jpg)

BCSE also owned a sister-company, IDM Integration, along with another business partner. From my first IDM project upgrading Shibboleth IDP in 2016, it was clear that I had a knack for identity engineering. I threw myself into IAM projects as often as I could, and by 2018 I was playing a pivotal role with IDMI, taking on a "senior" engineering role and being involved in much of the day-to-day operations of the business. We also rather quickly realized that I was more valuable consulting on identity and e-commerce projects, and closed up the small-business IT consulting largely by mid-2017.

In 2019, the third IDMI business partner chose to leave, and as a result we continued on as IDM Engineering. I took on a more formalized role in running the business, and since then I served as a main IDM Engineering engineer. Under my direction, IDME broadened its consulting specialization away from just Shibboleth-based SSO to **anything** IAM-related — embracing migrations from on-premises SAML implementations like Shibboleth and ADFS toward IDaaS offerings from Azure, Ping, and Okta.

That consulting work confirmed what I'd long suspected: identity and access management was exactly where I belonged. In late 2022, I had the opportunity to bring that expertise in-house at scale, joining the [University of Virginia](https://identity.virginia.edu) as an Identity Architect on their Identity Services team.

The work at UVA has been some of the most rewarding of my career. I led the rollout of [Internet2 Grouper](https://www.internet2.edu/products-services/trust-identity/grouper/), an enterprise group and access management platform now central to how the University manages authorization across the institution. I've taken on significant operational responsibility for UVA's [Shibboleth (NetBadge)](https://netbadge.virginia.edu) identity provider infrastructure, and I served as the senior engineer on the University's Privileged Access Management (PAM) deployment. Beyond those headline projects, I serve as a local subject matter expert for all things authentication and authorization: whether that means troubleshooting a gnarly SAML assertion, advising on an OIDC integration, or helping shape the direction of identity strategy at a university-wide scale. No two days are quite the same and I wouldn't have it any other way!

That's my story so far... Feel free to peek at my [LinkedIn](https://linkedin.com/in/kellenmurphy) or [GitHub](https://github.com/kellenmurphy) profiles, or [reach out to me directly](mailto:me@kellenmurphy.com) if you have questions!

Cheers!
— Kellen
